# Legal support in selected school closure reprieves

Checked 11 October 2026. Historical case selection: the 12 schools in the campaign's [existing other-schools research](../../lessons-data.json), compiled on 22 September 2026. [School-level CSV](cases.csv) · [Source register](sources.json).

Of those **12 selected reprieves, four (33.3%) have explicit evidence of lawyers, solicitors or a barrister acting for the school or its supporting diocese before the favourable outcome.** A broader count is **seven of 12 (58.3%) with documented legal representation or legal-advice activity**, including advice sought. The three additional cases report taking or seeking advice without identifying the provider; seeking advice does not itself confirm advice received or a lawyer retained. For five schools, neither lawyer representation nor legal-advice activity was established. Across all eight cases without explicit representation evidence, professional lawyer involvement remains unverified; none may be described as saved without lawyers.

These figures describe the cases already in our research. They do not measure lawyers' effectiveness or the proportion of all English schools saved with legal help.

## What was counted

The working interpretation of “saved after entering pre-consultation” includes reprieves at later stages, after a closure or site-merger consultation process had begun. The catalogue uses broad stage categories: it does not establish that every school entered a formally named, identical “pre-consultation” stage. St George's followed an academy trust consultation, which differs from the maintained-school process.

“Saved” means that the particular proposal stopped, or provision continued through an academy successor. It does not establish permanent protection or financial recovery. The historical outcome dates are retained; this audit is not a fresh register check proving that all 12 schools remain open today. Hazlewood faces a renewed proposal.

If the intended question is instead about schools **reprieved during initial consultation**, the existing catalogue contains three: Fletching, Godshill and St George's, Langton Matravers. One of three has reported legal advice (Godshill); none has explicit lawyer representation established in this audit. The other two are unknown, not confirmed negatives.

## Wording for legal-fund fundraising

> In our research into 12 school closure or merger reprieves, four had confirmed lawyer representation before the proposal was stopped. Three London schools had named solicitors or barristers involved when their closure proposals were overturned. Three more schools reported taking or seeking legal advice. Legal funds can help us obtain advice, check the evidence and challenge specific flaws in the process.

Keep the selection qualification with this wording. Do not replace it with “lawyers saved 58% of schools”, “hiring a lawyer gives a 58% success rate”, or a claim that legal spending caused these outcomes. Godshill's advice was reported as free; paid legal costs, advice intensity and the timing of spending are unknown for most cases. This evidence does not quantify a return on donations.

St John the Divine is a particularly clear example of actual representation: the source names both the barrister and instructing solicitor, while the adjudicator supplies the decision and its reasons. Kew is a community school; these Church of England cases' institutional referral routes cannot be assumed to be available to Kew parents.

## Method and remaining checks

- Reuse the existing 12 school IDs and their nine episode IDs. Keep the four selected closure comparisons outside this saved-school denominator. Do not expand the favourable-case set with search results selected because they mention lawyers.
- Code `legal_advice_or_representation=yes` for an attributed report of advice being taken/sought or explicit legal representation supporting the school before its historical favourable outcome. Code `explicit_lawyer=yes` only when the source actually identifies lawyers/solicitors/a barrister acting, rather than simply legal arguments or a referral process. Three advice-only cases do not establish professional credentials independently.
- Code missing evidence as `unknown`. Court involvement, the decision-maker's lawyers, a petition, a request for legal donations, or a programme-wide potential benefit cannot by themselves establish a school-side lawyer.
- Original council/OSA PDFs were downloaded outside the repository and inspected as text. New public statements and reporting were inspected through browsing. The source register distinguishes fresh inspections, inherited outcome evidence, and access failures. No full third-party documents, donor lists or private campaign correspondence are copied into this pack.
- Targeted public searches covered the school names with closure, lawyer, solicitor, barrister, legal advice and legal challenge terms. This was an evidence audit of a purposive catalogue, not a systematic national search. Source silence and unsuccessful searches cannot establish absence.
- Useful follow-up is to verify unnamed advice providers and the five unknowns, their work dates and whether support was paid or free. A genuine effectiveness estimate would need all eligible proposals in a defined area/period, including unsuccessful and unresolved cases, with common follow-up and lawyer involvement recorded before outcomes.
